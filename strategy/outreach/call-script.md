# Call and walk-in script

Not a script to read — a structure to stay inside. If you sound like you're reading, you've lost.

---

## Walk-in, 20 seconds

> "Hi — is the owner around? … {Name}, hi, I'm Nick, I'm local, over in Corona.
>
> I build AI systems for big companies during the week. I've built a small version that answers the
> phone for shops like yours when you're with a client.
>
> I called Thursday at 2pm and it rang out — that's probably a booking you didn't get.
>
> Don't take my word for it, just call this number right now and ask it something a customer would
> ask." *(hand over the card)*

Then **stop talking.** Let them call it. The silence while they listen is doing your job for you.

**Close:** "I'm not going to pitch you standing here. Can I call you Tuesday evening for fifteen
minutes?" — get a *time*, not a maybe.

---

## The 15-minute evening call

**1. Diagnose (5 min).** Ask, don't tell:
- "Walk me through what happens when the phone rings and you're with a client."
- "How many calls do you think you miss on a busy day?" *(They know. Let them say the number.)*
- "What happens to those people?" *(Answer is always: they call the next place.)*
- "What's a new client worth to you over a year?"

**That last question is the whole sale.** If a client is worth $600/year and they miss three calls a
week, the arithmetic makes the $349 obvious without you arguing for it. Let them do the maths out
loud. Don't do it for them.

**2. Show (3 min).** Have them call the demo again, this time asking a question specific to their
business. Then show a call-summary text on your phone.

**3. Scope (4 min).** Hours, services, prices, booking tool, what should escalate to their cell.
This doubles as the intake form and makes it feel already underway.

**4. Close (3 min).**
> "Setup's $995 and it's $349 a month. You're one of my first few here so I'll waive the setup — I
> want a testimonial and a referral or two out of it instead. I can have it live by {day}. Want me
> to start?"

Then be quiet. The first person to speak after the price loses.

---

## Objections

**"How much?"** — *(early)* "Depends on whether you want it just taking messages or actually booking.
Between $199 and $599 a month. Let me ask two things first so I don't quote you the wrong one."

**"I don't want a robot answering my phone."** — "Fair, and I'd hate a bad one too. It says up front
it's an assistant — it doesn't pretend to be a person. And it only picks up when *you* don't. Your
phone rings first, always. Call the demo and tell me honestly if it'd bother you as a customer."

**"My customers want a real person."** — "They want a booking. Right now they're getting voicemail —
that's not a real person either. This is voicemail that actually books them in."

**"I'll think about it."** — "Of course. What's the part you want to think about?" *(Real objection
lives here, every time.)*

**"Can it use my voice?"** — "Yes — that's the $599 tier. Takes about two minutes of recording."

**"What if it screws up?"** — "It forwards to your cell if it can't handle something, and you get a
text summary of every call, so you'll see anything odd within minutes. And you can turn it off from
your phone in one setting — it's forwarding, I never touch your actual number."

**"Are you going to be around?"** — "I run this alongside a contract with Microsoft — that's not
going away, and it's why I can build this properly. You get a written agreement, you own your setup,
and you can cancel any month."

---

## Never do

- Say the words "AI-powered," "solution," "transform," "leverage."
- Explain how it works. They don't care and every sentence adds doubt.
- Discount the monthly. Discount setup, and only in exchange for a testimonial or a referral.
- Promise a phone number for support. Promise an evening response, which you can keep.
- Pitch custom software on the first call. That comes at month two, from a paying client.
