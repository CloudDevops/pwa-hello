Questions I get asked

Do I have to change my phone number?

No. You keep your number. Calls that go unanswered — or all calls after hours, your choice — forward to a line I set up. Your phone always rings first. And because it's just call forwarding, you can turn the whole thing off yourself from your phone in one setting.

Will my customers know it's an AI?

Yes, and that's deliberate. The assistant says what it is at the start of the call. California requires that disclosure, and separately it's just good business — a caller who works out they were tricked into thinking they had a person leaves a bad review. In practice people don't mind. They mind voicemail.

What if it can't handle something?

It forwards the caller to your cell. A business phone line going quiet is the one failure you can't recover from, so it's built to hand off to a human rather than fail silently. You also get a text summary of every single call, so you'll spot anything odd within minutes rather than at the end of the week.

Does it work with my booking system?

Google Calendar, Square Appointments and most of the common salon and clinic tools, yes. If yours has an API it can be wired in. If it doesn't, the assistant collects the booking details and texts them to you — still faster than listening to voicemails at closing time.

How long does setup take?

About a week from the day you say go. I need one form from you — hours, services, prices, the questions customers ask most — and then roughly ten minutes of your time on the phone to switch the forwarding on.

What happens to my customers' information?

Call transcripts are encrypted, kept for a fixed window and then deleted. Nothing is used to train anything. It's written into the agreement, not just promised here.

What if I want to cancel?

Cancel any month. You turn the forwarding off and your phone behaves exactly as it did before. There's no contract term to escape and nothing of yours is held hostage.

Are you going to still be here in a year?

Fair question to ask a one-person business. I run this alongside a long-running enterprise engineering contract — that's not going away, and it's the reason I can build this properly rather than cheaply. You get a written agreement, and for custom work you own the code regardless.
