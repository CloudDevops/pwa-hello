How it actually works

No new hardware, no new phone number, nothing to install in your shop.

  • STEP 1 · YOUR PHONE STILL RINGS FIRST

    Calls forward only when you don't answer

    Your carrier forwards on the conditions you choose — no answer after four rings, busy, or after hours. Your published number never changes and you never port it. If you decide you hate it, you turn forwarding off in one setting on your phone and everything is back to how it was.

  • STEP 2 · IT ANSWERS LIKE SOMEONE WHO WORKS THERE

    It knows your hours, services, prices and quirks

    Because you told it, in the intake form, in your own words. "Do you take walk-ins?" "How much for a balayage?" "Are you open Sunday?" "Where do I park?" It says up front that it's an assistant — no pretending to be a person.

  • STEP 3 · IT BOOKS THEM IN

    Straight into your calendar, checking real availability

    Google Calendar, Square Appointments and most common salon and clinic booking tools. It confirms by text with the caller. If your system has no API, it collects the details and texts them to you instead — still better than a voicemail you listen to at closing.

  • STEP 4 · YOU GET THE SUMMARY

    A text on your phone, seconds after the call ends

    Who called, what they wanted, what was booked, and anything that needs you. You read it between customers instead of clearing voicemail at 8pm.

  • STEP 5 · WHEN IT CAN'T COPE, A HUMAN DOES

    It hands off rather than fumbles

    An angry customer, a complicated question, anything outside what it was given — it forwards to your cell. It's built to fail open, never to fail silent. A business phone that goes quiet is the one failure you can't undo.

What it doesn't do

Every vendor page tells you what a product does. Here's the other half, so you don't find out after you've paid.

It won't replace a receptionist

A good front-desk person reads the room, upsells, and remembers that Mrs. Alvarez likes the chair by the window. This answers the phone. That's a smaller job, done reliably.

It won't handle a genuinely upset customer

Nor should it. Those calls go to you, deliberately.

It only knows what you tell it

If your prices change and nobody updates it, it will quote the old ones. Changes are included — but somebody has to send them.

It isn't free to run

Voice AI costs real money per minute. That's why there's a minute allowance and why overage is billed at cost rather than hidden.

Questions I get asked

Is it legal for an AI to answer my business phone in California?

Yes, with two conditions, and both are built in. First, the assistant discloses that it's an automated assistant at the start of the call — California's bot-disclosure law requires that in a commercial context. Second, California is an all-party consent state for call recording, so callers are told up front if the call is recorded or transcribed. You don't have to remember any of this; it's in the configuration and in your agreement.

Can it really use my voice?

Yes, on the Pro tier — about two minutes of recording produces a clone that sounds like you. It still identifies itself as an assistant. Your voice makes it feel like your business; the disclosure keeps it honest. Both matter.

Where does it run? What if your computer goes down?

It runs on cloud infrastructure with monitoring and alerting, not on a machine in anyone's house. That's an obvious point that a surprising number of small AI vendors get wrong. If the system can't answer for any reason, calls fall through to your cell rather than disappearing.

What happens to my customers' data?

Transcripts are encrypted at rest, retained for a fixed window and then deleted. Nothing is used to train any model. Retention period and deletion are written into the agreement. If you're a clinic or med spa where calls might touch health information, tell me before we start — that changes how it's set up.
